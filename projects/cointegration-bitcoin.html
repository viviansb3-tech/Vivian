COINTEGRATION + BITCOIN VOLATILITY

MARKETS
IN MOTION.

A combined time-series study investigating the long-run equilibrium relationship between Brent and WTI crude oil, alongside Bitcoin price behaviour and volatility from January 2015 to 22 May 2026.

01
QUESTION

What happens beneath the price series?

The study asks whether Brent and WTI share a long-run equilibrium relationship, while examining trends, non-stationarity, forecasting behaviour, volatility clustering and heteroskedasticity in Bitcoin and oil-price returns.

02
DATA

Three financial series. Different behaviours.

The analysis compares Bitcoin, Brent crude oil and WTI crude oil. The supplied project report notes that Bitcoin has a different distribution, while Brent and crude oil show the same distribution. Bitcoin returns also display volatility clustering.

03
MODELLING · BITCOIN

ARIMA → ARCH → EGARCH

The supplied report states that Bitcoin supports AR(1) and MA(1) behaviour, with ARMA(1,1) suggesting underfitting or model instability. ACF and PACF of returns then motivate GARCH-family modelling.

Selected volatility model: EGARCH, reported as having the lowest AIC and BIC among the fitted volatility models.
04
MODELLING · OIL

Cointegration → ECM

The oil analysis addresses the risk of spurious regression using the Engle–Granger framework, then models the long-run equilibrium and short-run dynamics with an Error Correction Model.

Estimated long-run expression: Brent̂t = 1.6169 + 1.0469 WTÎt
ECM adjustment: the reported coefficient on ECTt−1 is −0.508197, indicating an adjustment toward the long-run relationship after disequilibrium.
05
INTERPRETATION

Why the models matter

The report concludes that Bitcoin volatility requires asymmetric modelling: the EGARCH result captures a leverage effect, with bad news producing stronger reactions. For oil, the findings support interconnected markets with long-run equilibrium and short-run dynamics.

06
CONCLUSION

From price movements to structure.

This project demonstrates a workflow from raw financial time series through distributional inspection, stationarity questions, model selection, volatility modelling and cointegration-based interpretation.
