Netcare + PPC
Cointegration & Volatility

A two-part financial time-series project: first testing whether Netcare and PPC share a stable long-run equilibrium, then modelling time-varying volatility in Netcare returns with ARIMA/GARCH-family methods.

Part 1 · Cointegration

ADF unit-root testing → Engle–Granger residual test → Johansen trace test → Error Correction Model.

Part 2 · Volatility

Log returns → stationarity and normality → ARIMA identification → ARCH testing → GARCH-family model comparison.

Research question

Do Netcare and PPC prices share a long-run relationship, and how quickly is disequilibrium corrected after shocks?

Volatility question

Which conditional-variance specification best captures persistence, clustering and asymmetry in Netcare returns?

Technical stack

  • R · tseries · urca · GARCH-family modelling
  • Augmented Dickey–Fuller testing
  • Engle–Granger and Johansen cointegration
  • Error Correction Model
  • ARIMA, ARCH diagnostics, sGARCH / eGARCH / GJR-GARCH / APARCH

Long-run equilibrium exists, but adjustment is slow — and volatility is asymmetric.

The two halves of the submitted project create one coherent econometric workflow from non-stationary price levels to stationary returns and conditional variance.

COINTEGRATION EVIDENCE

Both price levels were non-stationary, while first differences were stationary. The residual ADF test rejected a unit root (p = 0.0004), and the Johansen trace statistic of 36.55 exceeded the 5% critical value of 19.96.

ERROR CORRECTION

The lagged disequilibrium coefficient was reported as −0.0045, implying approximately 0.45% of a deviation from long-run equilibrium is corrected per period.

VOLATILITY EVIDENCE

Netcare log returns were stationary (ADF = −15.021). ARCH effects motivated conditional-variance modelling, and the preferred specification was GJR-GARCH(1,1) with Student-t innovations based on minimum BIC.

ASYMMETRY

The selected model reports persistence of 0.882 and a positive leverage estimate of 0.054, consistent with moderate persistence and a stronger volatility response to negative shocks.

REPORT-BACKED

The project demonstrates how different time-series questions require different models: cointegration for long-run co-movement and GARCH-family models for changing conditional variance.

ECONOMETRIC LOGIC

Non-stationary levels are tested before long-run modelling so spurious regression is avoided.

RISK LOGIC

Stationary returns are examined for serial dependence and ARCH effects before a volatility specification is selected.

MODEL SELECTION

Competing GARCH-family models are compared using information criteria, likelihood and residual diagnostics.

INTERPRETATION

The final model connects statistical persistence and leverage to market behaviour rather than presenting coefficients without context.
